How Cerebro works

Cerebro is a study assistant that runs on your own computer. It answers from the PDFs you choose and shows you where each answer came from.

From your PDF to an answer

  1. It reads the structure of your PDF

    Headings, paragraphs, lists, tables and page numbers are kept, so a topic stays whole instead of being cut at random points. Scanned pages without text are flagged, not guessed.

  2. It finds the passages that matter

    Your question is matched by meaning, by exact terms and by section titles, only inside the PDFs you selected. Follow-ups like "give me examples" stay on the topic you were discussing.

  3. The local model writes an explanation

    The model is told to teach rather than copy, and to put anything not found in your PDF under "Beyond your PDF".

  4. Every citation is checked

    Before you see the answer, each cited sentence is compared with the passage it points to. Citations that don't hold up are removed, and invented numbers or names are dropped.

  5. You practice

    Quizzes and flashcards are built from your sections. Each question needs proof from the text, and the model answers it independently to catch wrong answer keys.

What to keep in mind

Checks reduce mistakes; they don't make a small local model perfect. Open the cited page when an answer matters, especially for numbers and definitions you will be graded on. Scanned PDFs need OCR before Cerebro can read them.
